Research, not a recommendation

These are historical tests. They show what happened, not what will happen, and they say nothing about what you should buy. Results use public fund and index prices; trading costs and taxes are mostly not included, which would make the strategies look worse, not better.

How the tests were run

  • Rules first. The pass mark, the time window and the comparison were fixed before any results were seen.
  • One comparison. Every strategy is measured against a cheap fund that simply owns the market, because that is what any strategy has to beat to be worth the effort.
  • No second chances. A strategy that fails is recorded as failed. Its settings are not tweaked until it passes.
  • Known limits. Mostly US data, some samples are small, and the past is not the future. A pass is evidence, not proof.

The same idea runs through the rest of this site: write the forecast down first, then keep score in public. See the Scoreboard and how calibration works.
